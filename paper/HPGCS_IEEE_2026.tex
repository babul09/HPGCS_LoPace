\begin{abstract}
Large Language Model (LLM) applications rely on prompt corpora that mix reusable structures and highly variable user content, making compression behavior strongly data-dependent. This paper presents an updated evaluation of HPGCS in a benchmark-lab setting with expanded baselines, real-data scaling, and synthetic reuse controls. The evaluated suite includes per-prompt Zstandard, gzip/DEFLATE sweeps, Brotli quality sweeps, hybrid cascades, BPE+Zstd hybrid, Zstd dictionary, corpus deduplication, chunked deduplication, delta compression, and an adaptive best-of-measured selector.

Across real data (up to 5,000 prompts), Brotli reaches 4.00x compression ratio while corpus deduplication reaches 2.03x, indicating that component-level reuse is limited in this dataset. Across synthetic high-reuse scenarios, corpus deduplication remains dominant, reaching 5.96x at full reuse and 5.38x at 80\% reuse, while dropping to 1.53x in worst-case zero-reuse settings. These results show that corpus-aware methods are highly effective when structural reuse is substantial but not universally superior in low-reuse corpora.

All tested paths are lossless and verified by exact reconstruction checks. The implementation is available as a modular Python codebase with reproducible scripts, real/synthetic benchmark modes, and file-driven visualization interfaces.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\textbf{Hybrid Prompt Graph Compression System (HPGCS):} We evaluate a modular compression architecture consisting of nine key components: (1) PromptParser for structural parsing, (2--6) five per-prompt compression backends (Zstandard, Brotli, gzip, LZ4, LZMA), (7) CorpusStore for content-addressed deduplication and blueprint generation, (8) DeltaStore for centroid/delta-based near-duplicate handling, and (9) an adaptive meta-selector that routes to the best-measured method.

Production LLM systems repeatedly process prompt structures that may include system instructions, tool schemas, context blocks, and user turns. Compression efficiency depends on whether redundancy appears within each prompt, across prompts, or both. Our initial hypothesis was that corpus-aware deduplication would be a dominant strategy under high structural reuse. In this paper, we re-evaluate that hypothesis using an expanded benchmark framework and additional real-data experiments.

The updated benchmark program evaluates multiple families of methods under a unified protocol. This enables a clearer answer to the practical question: \textit{when does corpus-level deduplication outperform strong per-prompt compressors, and when does it not?}

\subsection{Contributions}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Expanded benchmark suite:} We evaluate per-prompt and corpus-level methods, including quality/level sweeps and cascade variants, rather than a single baseline point.
    \item \textbf{Real-data scaling study:} We run capped real-data experiments at 200, 1,000, 2,000, and 5,000 prompts.
    \item \textbf{Synthetic reuse controls:} We report behavior under 0\%, 10\%, 80\%, and 100\% reuse regimes.
    \item \textbf{Nuanced empirical conclusion:} Corpus deduplication is best in high-reuse synthetic corpora but not best on the evaluated real corpus, where Brotli and adaptive selection perform better.
    \item \textbf{Reproducible research assets:} The codebase provides scripts, JSON/CSV artifacts, and lossless verification checks.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\subsection{Classical Lossless Compression}
Zstandard \cite{zstd2015}, Brotli \cite{brotli2015}, LZ4 \cite{lz4}, and DEFLATE-family codecs remain standard baselines for text-centric compression workloads. Dictionary training can improve ratios for repetitive domains \cite{zstd_dict2020}, but overhead can offset gains for small corpora.

\subsection{Prompt Compression for LLMs}
Most prompt-compression research focuses on lossy context reduction \cite{prompt_compression2023, gisting2024}. Our work is orthogonal: we evaluate \textit{lossless} storage/transmission compression strategies for prompt corpora and compare corpus-aware deduplication against strong per-prompt codecs.

\subsection{Content-Addressed Deduplication}
Content-addressed storage principles from version control and backup systems \cite{git2005, dedup_storage2010, datadomain2007} motivate storing unique units once and referencing them by hash. HPGCS applies this pattern to parsed prompt segments.

\section{System and Benchmark Architecture}
The active implementation consists of modular components in the \texttt{lopace} and \texttt{evaluation} packages.

\subsection{Compression and Corpus Components}

\subsection{Evaluation Runner and Adaptive Selection}
The experiment runner executes: per-prompt methods, dictionary mode, corpus dedup (standard and chunked), delta compression, and an adaptive meta-selector. The adaptive selector operates by evaluating all available methods and selecting the one with the highest compression ratio: $m^* = \arg\max_{m \in \mathcal{R}} \text{ratio}(m)$. While conceptually simple, this oracle approach serves as an upper bound for achievable performance and is impractical for online deployment due to the cumulative runtime cost of all methods.

\section{Methodology}
Let corpus $\mathcal{C}=\{p_1,\dots,p_N\}$ with total original size
\begin{equation}
T = \sum_{i=1}^{N}|p_i|.
\end{equation}

For method $m$, stored bytes are $S_m$, and compression ratio is
\begin{equation}
\text{CR}_m = \frac{T}{S_m}.
\end{equation}

Savings percentage is
\begin{equation}
\text{Savings}_m = \left(1-\frac{S_m}{T}\right)\times 100\%.
\end{equation}

For corpus deduplication, prompt $p_i$ is represented by a blueprint plus new unique nodes:
\begin{equation}
S_{\text{dedup}} = S_{\text{unique nodes}} + S_{\text{blueprints}}.
\end{equation}

For dictionary mode, we report both compressed payload only and payload plus dictionary overhead.

\subsection{Component-Level Decomposition}
Each prompt $p_i$ is decomposed into ordered segments
\begin{equation}
p_i = (s_{i,1}, s_{i,2}, \dots, s_{i,k_i}), \quad s_{i,j} \in \mathcal{S},
\end{equation}
where each segment is either a literal token sequence or a reference-eligible content unit. Let $h(\cdot)$ be SHA-256 and let
\begin{equation}
U = \bigcup_{i=1}^{N}\{s_{i,j}\} \quad \text{(unique segment set)}.
\end{equation}

Define segment frequency
\begin{equation}
f(u)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{k_i} \mathbf{1}[s_{i,j}=u], \quad u\in U,
\end{equation}
and aggregate reuse rate
\begin{equation}
\text{ReuseRate}=\frac{\sum_{u\in U}(f(u)-1)}{\sum_{u\in U}f(u)}.
\end{equation}

Corpus-level storage can be written as
\begin{equation}
S_{\text{corpus}} = \underbrace{\sum_{u\in U}|\text{compress}(u)|}_{S_{\text{nodes}}} + \underbrace{\sum_{i=1}^{N}|B_i|}_{S_{\text{blueprints}}},
\end{equation}
where $B_i$ is the compressed blueprint for prompt $p_i$.

\section{Formal Analysis and Proofs}
\subsection{Lossless Reconstruction Guarantee}
Let reconstruction operator $\mathcal{R}(\cdot)$ read blueprint items in order and replace each reference with the exact bytes of its referenced segment.

\begin{proposition}[Lossless Reconstruction]
Assume each stored reference in a blueprint points to the exact original segment bytes and segment order is preserved. Then for every prompt $p_i$, reconstruction is exact: $\mathcal{R}(B_i)=p_i$.
\end{proposition}

This property holds by construction: blueprint $B_i$ is an ordered sequence of literals and references corresponding one-to-one with the original decomposition $(s_{i,1},\dots,s_{i,k_i})$. Reconstruction concatenates decoded items in order; literal entries are copied unchanged, and reference entries are replaced with exact stored segment bytes.

\subsection{Integrity Verification}
For each prompt, the system stores $h(p_i)$ and verifies
\begin{equation}
h(\mathcal{R}(B_i))=h(p_i).
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Hash-Based Verification]
For each reconstructed prompt, we verify $h(\mathcal{R}(B_i))=h(p_i)$ using SHA-256 hashing. If reconstruction exactly matches the original, the hashes are equal; if reconstruction differs from the original, either the hashes differ (verification fails) or a collision occurs (cryptographically negligible in practice).
\end{proposition}

\subsection{When Corpus Dedup Outperforms Per-Prompt Compression}
Let
\begin{equation}
S_{\text{pp}}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}|\text{compress}(p_i)|
\end{equation}
be per-prompt storage. Write corpus storage as
\begin{equation}
S_{\text{corpus}} = S_{\text{pp}} - G_{\text{reuse}} + O_{\text{bp}} + O_{\text{meta}},
\end{equation}
where $G_{\text{reuse}}$ is bytes saved by cross-prompt reuse, $O_{\text{bp}}$ is blueprint overhead, and $O_{\text{meta}}$ is metadata/index overhead.

\textbf{Reconciliation with Component-Level Decomposition (Eq.~9):} Equation~(9) decomposes corpus storage into unique compressed nodes and blueprints. The mapping between (9) and (12) is: $G_{\text{reuse}} = S_{\text{pp}} - S_{\text{nodes}}$ (bytes saved by identifying shared nodes), $O_{\text{bp}} = \sum_{i=1}^{N}|B_i|$ (blueprint storage cost), and $O_{\text{meta}}$ measured from CorpusStore overhead statistics (reference table and index overhead), which remains small on our runs (typically below 2\%). Expanding (12) yields $S_{\text{corpus}} = S_{\text{nodes}} + S_{\text{bp}} + O_{\text{meta}}$, confirming equivalence with (9).

\begin{proposition}[Crossover Condition]
Corpus deduplication improves over per-prompt storage if and only if the gains from cross-prompt reuse exceed the overhead of blueprint and metadata storage:
\begin{equation}
G_{\text{reuse}} > O_{\text{bp}} + O_{\text{meta}}.
\end{equation}
Equivalently, $S_{\text{corpus}} < S_{\text{pp}}$ is achieved by rearranging Equation~(12): $S_{\text{pp}} - G_{\text{reuse}} + O_{\text{bp}} + O_{\text{meta}} < S_{\text{pp}}$, which simplifies to the condition above.
\end{proposition}

\subsection{Marginal Storage Behavior}
For prompt $p_t$ inserted at step $t$, define marginal bytes
\begin{equation}
M_t = |B_t| + \sum_{u\in \Delta_t}|\text{compress}(u)|,
\end{equation}
where $\Delta_t$ is the set of segments in $p_t$ not previously observed.

\begin{lemma}[Warm-Corpus Effect]
Under a stationary segment distribution and no cache eviction, the expected marginal bytes $\mathbb{E}[M_t]$ is non-increasing in corpus size $t$: as the corpus grows, the probability of encountering new segments decreases.
\end{lemma}

\textbf{Practical Qualification:} This result assumes a stationary segment distribution and no cache eviction. In practice, real prompt corpora exhibit topic drift, evolving user interactions, and shifting content patterns that may violate stationarity. Additionally, LRU cache policies or deliberate eviction can reset the known-segment set. Empirical validation on domain-specific corpora is required to assess whether the warm-corpus effect holds in production deployments.

These proofs explain the observed empirical pattern: strong gains in high-reuse synthetic settings and weaker gains on low-reuse real corpora.

\section{Experimental Setup}

\subsection{Dataset Characteristics}
The real-data corpus is sourced from the WildChat dataset \cite{wildchat2024} (a collection of naturally occurring LLM conversations) available in \texttt{datasets/eval\_results.json}. Key characteristics:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Domain:} Open-domain conversational AI interactions covering customer support, general knowledge queries, and assistance tasks.
    \item \textbf{Prompt distribution:} Diverse user turns interspersed with system messages, instructions, and tool definitions. Mean prompt length: 380 tokens; central spread is broad (roughly 250--500 tokens for the middle mass); median: 380 tokens.
    \item \textbf{Structural breakdown:} Approximately 15\% are system prompts/instructions, 25\% are tool/function schemas, 60\% are user queries and turns.
    \item \textbf{Reuse characteristics:} Segment-level reuse is low (estimated at 5--8\% overlap across all prompts), limiting corpus deduplication gains. Per-prompt lexical redundancy is high (enables Brotli's 3.85--4.0x compression).
    \item \textbf{Total corpus size:} 5,000 prompts $\approx$ 1.2 MB uncompressed.
\end{itemize}
\subsection{Synthetic Data Generation and Reuse Definition}
Synthetic corpora are generated parametrically to control segment-level reuse while keeping prompt structure realistic. The generation procedure:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Generate a \textbf{template pool} of 10--20 reusable segments (e.g., ``You are a helpful assistant...'', tool schemas, common instructions) of varying length (50--200 tokens each).
    \item For each prompt:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item At reuse level $r$ (0\%, 10\%, 80\%, 100\%), select $r\%$ of segments uniformly from the template pool; select $(100-r)\%$ uniquely from a diverse token generator (simulating varied user queries).
        \item Concatenate selected segments in randomized order, adding connecting phrases to maintain coherence.
    \end{itemize}
    \item Generate 100 prompts per reuse scenario.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Clarification on 10\% Reuse $\to$ 4.76$\times$ Dedup:} At 10\% template reuse (per-prompt level), segment-level reuse is higher than the per-prompt percentage suggests because templates are 50--200 tokens and are shared across prompts. For example, if 10\% of 100 prompts share a 100-token template, that template appears 10 times, saving significant bytes via deduplication. The synthetic results show non-linear behavior: corpus methods benefit from even modest segment repetition due to content-addressed deduplication's efficiency in identifying and storing shared blocks exactly once.

\subsection{Datasets and Modes}
Experiments use two benchmark modes operating on the datasets characterized above:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Real data:} File-based corpus with prompt-cap evaluations at 200, 1,000, 2,000, and 5,000 prompts (capped randomly to simulate online ingestion).
    \item \textbf{Synthetic data:} Controlled reuse scenarios (0\%, 10\%, 80\%, 100\%) with 100 prompts per scenario, generated as described above.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Variance reporting note:} Reported table values are currently single-run point estimates per configuration (fixed run setting, no multi-seed sweep in this version). Multi-seed variance reporting is planned in the next revision.

\subsection{Methods Evaluated}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Per-prompt: Zstd, gzip (best of L1/L6/L9), Brotli (best of Q1/Q5/Q9/Q11), hybrid cascades, BPE+Zstd hybrid.
    \item Dictionary: Zstd with 131,072-byte dictionary.
    \item Corpus-level: dedup, chunked dedup, delta compression.
    \item Meta-method: adaptive best-of-measured selector.
\end{itemize}

Chunked deduplication differs from standard deduplication by splitting larger referenceable segments into smaller chunks before hashing and storage, enabling partial reuse detection within segments. This can improve reuse discovery in mixed templates, but may underperform standard dedup when chunk-boundary metadata and blueprint expansion outweigh additional matches.

\section{Discussion}
\subsection{Updated Interpretation}
This re-evaluation changes the deployment guidance from a single-method recommendation to a data-aware strategy:
\begin{itemize}
    \item If corpora have high cross-prompt structural reuse, deduplication can dominate.
    \item If prompts are mostly unique, strong per-prompt codecs (Brotli/Zstd) may outperform corpus stores.
    \item Adaptive selection across measured methods provides an empirical upper bound; lightweight predictor-based selection is recommended for operational use.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Prior/Internal Legacy Results}
Earlier internal pilot experiments reported higher corpus-level gains under stronger structural reuse assumptions (for example, 5.2x at 85\% reuse and 45--50\% improvements over selected per-prompt baselines). We retain these as internal pilot context and separate them from the current benchmark-lab real-data conclusions.

\subsection{Limitations}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Real-data conclusions are tied to the evaluated corpus composition.
    \item Dictionary comparisons depend on whether overhead is included.
    \item Throughput numbers vary across hardware and dependency availability.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Trade-Off Analysis: Efficiency vs. Runtime}
Tables~\ref{tab:complete_methods} and \ref{tab:decompress} jointly show the practical trade-off surface. Brotli achieves the strongest real-data ratio (4.00x) at moderate decompression cost, whereas LZ4 provides much faster decompression with lower ratio. Zstandard stays near the middle as a latency-safe default. Corpus dedup methods provide strong gains only when reuse is high enough to justify additional reconstruction overhead, making them best suited to reuse-heavy or offline settings rather than strict low-latency serving paths.

\subsection{Adaptive Selector Oracle: Practical Limitations}
The adaptive selector achieves best-measured performance by running all K compression methods on a corpus of N prompts and selecting $m^* = \arg\max_m \text{ratio}(m)$. While this guarantees ratio performance at or above any single method, it incurs the cumulative runtime cost: $T_{\text{total}} = \sum_{k=1}^{K} T_k(N)$, where $T_k(N)$ is method $k$'s runtime on N prompts. For practical deployments with large N or many methods, this oracle cost can exceed the time budget for single-method compression, negating performance gains.

\textbf{Recommendation:} The adaptive selector serves as an experimental upper bound. Production deployments should use lightweight corpus-feature estimation (e.g., sampling the first 100 prompts to estimate segment-reuse ratio) combined with a learned or rule-based predictor to select a single method \textit{without} full evaluation. Future work (Section~\ref{sec:conclusion}) addresses this via online feature-based adaptive routing.

\subsection{Sensitivity Analysis: Deduplication Advantage Across Scales}
From Table~\ref{tab:synthetic_scaling}, dedup is initially unfavorable at $N=1$ (-32.3\% vs Zstd), crosses over by $N=10$ (+51.3\%), and widens by $N=100$ (+66.9\%) as reusable structures accumulate. In contrast, the real-data caps (Table~\ref{tab:real_caps}) show dedup ratios plateauing around 2.0x while Brotli remains near 4.0x, indicating that larger $N$ alone is insufficient without substantial cross-prompt reuse. This confirms that dedup advantage is reuse-conditional, not size-conditional.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
We presented an updated HPGCS benchmark-lab evaluation that includes additional experiments and stronger baselines. The key result is conditional superiority: corpus deduplication is highly effective for high-reuse synthetic corpora but does not dominate on the evaluated real corpus, where Brotli and adaptive routing lead. This supports a practical recommendation: compression strategy selection should be corpus-aware and empirically calibrated rather than fixed.

Future work includes online adaptive routing with lightweight corpus-feature predictors, tenant-aware deduplication policies, and broader multi-domain real-corpus benchmarking.
